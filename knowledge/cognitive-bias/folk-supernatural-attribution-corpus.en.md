# Folk Supernatural Attribution Corpus: Hearsay Chains, Perceptual Self-Exemption, and Self-Sealing Formulas

## 0. Scope Restriction & Ontological Neutrality

This entry follows the repository's scope axiom: the object of analysis is **utterance text**, not **human beings**. Every criterion below is decided from the text alone, and inferring a speaker's identity, region, occupation, education, class, or psychological state is **prohibited** (see the Scope Restriction in [`RUBRIC.en.md`](../../skills/source-evaluator/RUBRIC.en.md)).

This entry additionally restricts itself in two ways:

1. **It makes no judgement about whether supernatural phenomena exist.** What is analysed is **whether the argument structure of an attribution is testable**, not what the world is made of. Claiming "X does not exist" requires evidence just as much as claiming "X exists"; this repository supplies neither and does not attempt to. Where a formula like "science cannot explain it" is flagged, the reason is that it **inverts the burden of proof** (unexplained ≠ non-existent)—not that its conclusion is false.
2. **It does not disparage folk empirical knowledge wholesale.** Oral tradition can genuinely encode real environmental and safety information (e.g. "don't go to that pond" may track a real drowning hazard). What is criticised here is the **form**—provenance evaporation, post-hoc attribution, self-sealing—not the claim that oral content is uniformly false. An untestable form and an occasionally true content can hold at the same time.

---

## 1. Family Definition

**Folk Supernatural Attribution Corpus**: a class of discourse that presents untestable causal attributions as settled fact, scaffolded by **hearsay**, **perceptual self-exemption**, **temporal contiguity**, and **self-sealing formulas**.

Its shared trait is not "it talks about the supernatural" but that **its argument structure withstands no possible observation**: whatever is observed, the conclusion does not change.

## 2. Cluster Taxonomy

| Cluster | Utterances | Structural Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A Hearsay chain & provenance evaporation** | 《听说那人死了》·《村里老人说》·《大家都说是招惹了》·《后来问家人才知道是》 | Each retelling strips a layer of provenance; the terminal clause cites "everyone / the elders / family"—an anonymous collective that cannot be cross-examined |
| **B Perceptual & memory self-exemption** | 《很小的时候》·《没太看清楚》·《醒来什么都不记得》 | The narrator declares that no valid observation occurred, yet draws a definite conclusion from it—evidentiary strength inverted against conclusion strength |
| **C Temporal contiguity posing as causation** | 《第二天就发高烧了》 | Causation supported by temporal adjacency alone; no base rate, no control |
| **D Built-in unfalsifiability (self-sealing)** | 《信则有不信则无》·《科学不能解释不代表没有》·《有些东西有说法》 | The proposition is constructed so no observation can refute it; the burden of proof is often inverted |
| **E Cost asymmetry treated as a truth-criterion** | 《宁可信其有，不可信其无》 | Wager structure: expected decision payoff substituted for factual truth-assessment |
| **F Disqualification & the age gate** | 《那是你没见过》·《等你长大了就知道了》 | A unilateral verdict on the other party's standing; or knowledge gated by age instead of by evidence |

Cluster F's 《那是你没见过》 is **structurally identical** to the carrier-independent disqualification archived in [`zero-information-disqualification-cases.en.md`](./zero-information-disqualification-cases.en.md): an unevidenced challenge to competence substituted for an argument about the truth of the issue.

## 3. Deconstruction

### 3.1 Cluster A: hearsay chains and provenance evaporation (Sperber, 1996; Fei Xiaotong, 1948)
Representations are not copied intact in transmission; they are reshaped by **memorability and transmissibility**: the more a saying needs no source, no cross-check, and the tighter its structure, the better it survives the chain. Each link strips a layer of checkability, until the terminal clause offers only "everyone / the elders / family"—a collective that **cannot be summoned as a witness**. This is isomorphic to the "Great Buddha" of [`appeal-to-phantom-authority.en.md`](./appeal-to-phantom-authority.en.md), with the national authority swapped for a village-scale one.

There is exactly one testable follow-up question: **can the terminal source be named and checked?** If not, the narrative's transmission strength says nothing about its truth—only about its transmissibility.

### 3.2 Cluster B: perceptual self-exemption and the evidence-conclusion inversion (Loftus, 2005)
"No, I didn't see it clearly", "I remembered nothing when I woke up", "when I was very small" are all declarations that **the narrator's own observation was invalid**. In ordinary inference an invalid observation should suspend the conclusion; here the opposite happens—the blurrier the observation, the more definite the conclusion. This is not carelessness but self-exemption: **"I cannot supply evidence" is rewritten as "evidence cannot be supplied"**, pre-emptively closing off inquiry.

As for childhood recollection, its unreliability is not a matter of personal character but a general property of memory: misinformation-planting studies show childhood recollections are highly susceptible to later suggestion (Loftus, 2005). "When I was very small" therefore has close to zero evidentiary force, and no amount of subjective conviction repairs it.

### 3.3 Cluster C: temporal contiguity posing as causation (Nisbett & Ross, 1980; Tversky & Kahneman, 1974)
"He had a high fever the next day" supplies **temporal adjacency** only. Causal inference needs **covariation evidence**: is there a control, what is the base rate (fever is common), does it disappear when the cause is removed, is there a dose-response relation. Substituting adjacency for covariation is a systematic shortcut in human causal attribution (Nisbett & Ross, 1980), typically accompanied by base-rate neglect (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974).

The difference from a genuine medical narrative lies not in the conclusion but in **whether a checkable test is supplied**: naming a pathogen and a test constitutes a falsifiable causal claim; offering only sequence does not.

### 3.4 Cluster D: self-sealing and burden-of-proof inversion (Popper, 1959; Walton, 1996)
- 《信则有不信则无》 relocates the proposition **into the believer's mind**: it is then no longer a claim about the world but a claim about belief—no observation can reach it.
- 《科学不能解释不代表没有》 is the standard form of **argument from ignorance**: from "not yet explained" it derives "exists", while shifting the burden of proof from the claimant to the sceptic (Walton, 1996).
- 《有些东西有说法》 substitutes a **vague reference** ("there are sayings") for a concrete proposition. No proposition, no testable object.

All three share one consequence: **no possible observation could refute the proposition**, so it leaves the domain of empirical discussion altogether (Popper, 1959). This is the same family as [`in-theory-hedging-trap.en.md`](./in-theory-hedging-trap.en.md) (unfalsifiable escape), differing only in carrier: one runs on "in theory", the other on "if you believe, it's there".

### 3.5 Cluster E: cost asymmetry mistaken for a truth-criterion (Pascal, 1670)
《宁可信其有，不可信其无》 is itself a **decision heuristic**, and in situations of scarce information and irreversible loss the precautionary principle has a legitimate place. The fallacy lies in the **misappropriation**: treating "this is the safer choice" as "this is what is true".

Decision theory answers "what should I do"; truth theory answers "what is the case". The two may return different answers without conflicting. Passing off the former as the latter is the wager structure (Pascal, 1670): replacing factual assessment with the arithmetic of expected payoff.

### 3.6 Cluster F: disqualification and the age-gated theory of knowledge
- 《那是你没见过》 is a **carrier-independent unilateral disqualification**: it names no mechanism and lands on the other party's insufficient experience. See [`zero-information-disqualification-cases.en.md`](./zero-information-disqualification-cases.en.md) §1.
- 《等你长大了就知道了》 sets the admission condition for knowledge to **age** rather than evidence. It is a thought-terminating cliché: it halts the discussion and waives the burden of proof at once. This is isomorphic to the gerontocratic domination recorded in [`anthropoid-lifecycle/`](../anthropoid-lifecycle/index.en.md), with the domain shifted from resource allocation to epistemology: **age is not evidence for a claim**, and no age constitutes proof.

## 4. Criteria & Protocol

### 4.1 Decidable at L1 from the text alone: self-sealing formulas
The complete formulas of clusters D and F (《信则有不信则无》《科学不能解释不代表没有》《有些东西有说法》《宁可信其有，不可信其无》《等你长大了就知道了》《那是你没见过》) are **complete rhetorical moves that do not depend on context**, and are therefore hard-cut at L1:

- **Flag**: `FLAG_SELF_SEALING_UNFALSIFIABLE`
- **Conjunction**: a self-sealing formula is present **and** the text contains no mechanism or measurement marker (if a mechanism is also named, the text carries substance alongside the formula and is not cut)
- **Sibling flag**: `FLAG_IN_THEORY_UNFALSIFIABLE_HEDGE` (the "in theory" carrier)

### 4.2 Not decidable by regex: clusters A/B/C, deferred to L2/L3 narrative review
《听说》《大家都说》《村里老人说》《没太看清楚》《醒来什么都不记得》《很小的时候》《第二天就发高烧了》 are **innocuous in isolation**—"I heard this restaurant is good" may be followed by a checkable source. They become fallacious only inside a **whole narrative**, and regex cannot adjudicate narrative structure. These three clusters are therefore **explicitly deferred to L2/L3 human or model review**, with a common inquiry checklist:

1. **Source**: is a checkable source named? An anonymous collective ("everyone / the elders / family") means provenance has evaporated.
2. **Observation**: does the narrator claim to have observed anything valid? "Didn't see it clearly / don't remember" is an admission that no valid observation occurred.
3. **Covariation**: is the conclusion supported by temporal adjacency alone? Is a base rate or a control given?
4. **Falsifiability**: what possible observation would refute the proposition? If none can be stated, it is self-sealing.
5. **Decision vs. truth**: is a "safer choice" being passed off as a "factual finding"?

## 6. Boundaries & Open Questions

1. **Ontological neutrality**: this entry makes no judgement about the truth of any supernatural proposition, and constitutes no evaluation of communities holding folk beliefs (§0). The §7 Irkutsk case follows the same discipline: it records **whether the notification-and-retelling structure is testable** and **does not adjudicate** the victim's medical cause of death (that is a clinical and microbiological question, not this entry's carrier).
2. **Form/content separation**: the target is argumentative form (provenance evaporation, perceptual exemption, post-hoc attribution, self-sealing); the possibility that oral tradition encodes real risk information is not excluded, nor is the possibility that an institutional script was ever accurate.
3. **Falsifiable propositions awaiting test**: whether the fallacy rate of clusters A/B/C declines as narrators acquire checkable instruments (recording devices, medical tests, water-quality assays); and whether the media chain invoked in §7 reproduces in later biological and chemical press events. Both are observable behavioural-trajectory claims and may be retained as hypotheses subject to testing.
4. **Known detection gap**: the eight `ACCEPTED` rows of §5 are a **known and deliberately retained** gap. Closing it depends on narrative-review capability, not on expanding the regex table—expansion would only introduce collateral damage.

## 7. Cross-Cluster Case: Irkutsk APRI Plague-Research-Institute Staff Death (2026-10)

This section does not archive "plague" itself as a biological phenomenon. It demonstrates **how the retelling chain replays at national scale as a master-branch pre-announcement on social media, while the official account lags.** The event's underlying ontology wrongness—natural-focus pneumonia, laboratory exposure, zoonotic spillover, or Mycoplasma—is outside this entry's remit. Its comment-space is that official communications **denied, revised, and fell silent multiple times within the first 48 hours**, while Chinese and international media and social platforms ran several independent supernatural and conspiratorial restructurings down clusters A→B→C→D.

### 7.1 Verified factual base (as of 2026-10-10; sources in frontmatter and inline)
- **Institution**: Irkutsk Antiplague Research Institute (Irkutsk APRI, 伊尔库茨克西伯利亚与远东防鼠疫研究所), a centre whose research portfolio includes dangerous pathogens including plague, sited where plague enzootics occur naturally.
- **Person**: Darya Shipilova, a 28-year-old junior researcher (lab assistant) at APRI.
- **Timeline**:
  - 2026-09-25: the victim handles **Yersinia pestis** in the laboratory and is exposed—most Chinese secondary versions say a culture tube shattered, and record it without independent verification; the substantiated core is only that she died of severe pneumonia/respiratory distress.
  - 2026-09-29: presents with severe pneumonic symptoms and is admitted to hospital.
  - 2026-10-02: dies.
  - 2026-10-02 (same afternoon): WHO's internal monitoring first picks up the suspected death via **media reports** and requests verification from the Russian health authorities (UN Geneva press briefing, 7 Oct).
  - 2026-10-05: Rospotrebnadzor rewrites the cause to **"pneumonia of undetermined etiology"**, explicitly stating **no plague pathogen detected**; the Buryatia Republic administration initially cites health-regulator information **referencing plague**, then retracts; ~200 contacts are placed under medical observation.
  - 2026-10-06: US Secretary of State Marco Rubio publicly confirms US monitoring of the research-centre death.
  - 2026-10-07: WHO spokesperson Christian Lindmeier tells Geneva press that **Russian health authorities have not yet confirmed the cause of death**; the illness "initially presented as a typical acute respiratory viral infection"; contacts have "no dangerous infectious pathogens detected"; the IHR notification process is formally activated. The same day CNN publishes: *Russian health watchdog says there is no risk for epidemic while WHO says they need more information*.
  - 2026-10-09: Chinese microblog posts emerge with headings like "the plague has flared" and "WHO faces widening doubts", and official rebuttals begin to circulate; Beijing reports no contact with Moscow beyond routine border quarantine channels. UC Riverside publishes a Q&A explaining pneumonic plague basics for general readers.
  - 2026-10-10: WHO releases formal disease-outbreak news **DON620**, headlined *Pneumonia of undetermined etiology - Russian Federation*. Conclusion: the cause is not yet confirmed; monitoring and verification continue.
  - 2026-10-13: some Russian outlets begin reporting that the preventive epidemic-control measures in Irkutsk have been **lifted**, with the local situation described as stable and posing no public-health risk.

### 7.2 Position in this framework: a national-scale retelling chain
| Village-scale version (§3.1, Fei Xiaotong–Sperber) | This event's news-chain version |
| :--- | :--- |
| 《听说那人死了》heard at the dinner table from the neighbour | a WeChat/Weibo user screenshots a post saying "big news over in Russia" |
| 《后来问家人才知道是鼠疫》 | a million-follower account relays a "breaking news" item: "a researcher died; what she contracted is unclear" |
| 《大家都说不能去北边那家厂》 | five influential accounts in unison advise "avoid Siberia" (without any epidemiological guidance document) |
| 《我问村长他也不说》 | Reuters/WHO fields the same question repeatedly and gets only "temporarily unable to confirm" |

Each step keeps **three falsifiable forms open at once**: true statement (grounded in fact), false statement (narrative drift), and undecidable statement (provenance evaporated). The reader ends up facing one of the three, but the disseminator pays no equivalent certainty cost.

### 7.3 What separates this event from the village version: media-architecture asymmetry magnifies Cluster A
- Village version: short chain; provenance evaporates within two or three person-to-person retellings; one named journalist or village head can terminate it.
- This event: **the source was originally not any Russian official channel**. WHO first learnt of the incident from **social-media coverage** (UN Geneva briefing, 7 Oct: *"the internal monitoring system picked up the media reports"*). The information then passed through 4–7 hops: agency press-conference recording, numbered document DOCS, wire-service repost, short-video caption, blogger commentary. Each hop carries an editable window. In the end "the village elders all say" is rewritten as "all the Chinese social-media accounts say".

Cluster A therefore holds not only for oral intergenerational transmission, **but also for the page traffic that follows a national biosafety press conference that has broken down**.

### 7.4 Criteria for classifying this event as Cluster A rather than C/D/E
- Not Cluster C (temporal causation): no serious version stops at "she fell ill → she died"; anything giving sequence must connect to test ID or decree number.
- Not Cluster D (unfalsifiable): control measures were eventually lifted, contact tests found "no dangerous pathogens", WHO publicly continues to seek confirmation. If it cannot notify "this CDC release number has been issued", it cannot be wrapped into a conclusion.
- Not Cluster E (wager structure): there is no cost-substitution reasoning of the form "better safe than sorry, evacuate now". The case must pass the structural test to stand.

Emphasis: this entry does not claim medical-causal authority; **this section records only the structure of the retelling chain**.
