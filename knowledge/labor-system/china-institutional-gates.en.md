# China's Labor System: Systematic Age Gatekeeping

## 1. Legal Framework: Institutional Tolerance of Age Discrimination

### 1.1 The Legislative Blind Spot

The *Labor Law of the People's Republic of China* (1994) states in Article 12:
> "Workers shall not be discriminated against in employment based on ethnicity, race, gender, or religious belief."

**Age is conspicuously absent from the enumerated anti-discrimination categories.**

China has no dedicated national-level age discrimination in employment statute comparable to the US Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA, 1967) or the EU Employment Equality Directive (2000/78/EC). This legislative vacuum provides the default legal permissibility for institutionalizing the 35-year hiring cutoff at scale.

### 1.2 Civil Service Examination Age Caps

The Civil Service Examination Regulations set a binding age ceiling of **18–35 years** for most positions, with limited exceptions for doctoral degree holders (extendable to 40):

1. **Institutional normalization**: The administrative age ceiling establishes "35 = standard labor force expiry date" as a collective institutional default across all employing entities that use civil service norms as benchmarks.
2. **Isomorphic diffusion**: State-owned enterprises and large institutional employers systematically replicate the 35-year cutoff, generating cross-sector institutional isomorphism.
3. **Self-reinforcing stigma**: As the cutoff becomes universal practice, workers internalize "career expiry" anxiety, further legitimizing the discriminatory norm.

## 2. The "Fresh Graduate" System: An Institutional Economics Analysis

### 2.1 The Exclusionary Design of Fresh-Graduate Status

"Fresh-graduate" (应届生) status in China's labor market carries exceptional institutional privileges:

| Policy Domain | Fresh-Graduate Advantage | Exclusion for Non-Fresh Graduates |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Urban Hukou (residency) allocation** | Priority residency access in core cities within 2 years of graduation | Any employment record before obtaining hukou results in immediate disqualification |
| **State-owned enterprise campus recruitment** | Exclusive campus recruiting sessions | Non-fresh graduates restricted to general social recruitment with inferior positions |
| **Civil service specialized cohort plans** | Dedicated fresh-graduate recruitment tracks in some provinces | Workers with employment records excluded from applying |
| **Guaranteed postgraduate admission** | Undergraduate GPA-based direct doctoral pathway | Re-entry after employment only through open-exam competition with inferior resources |

### 2.2 Path Dependency and Lock-in Effects

This institutional design replicates **technological lock-in**:
- Any departure from the "fresh-graduate" pipeline (due to sabbatical, entrepreneurial ventures, career exploration, or family caretaking) permanently voids access to institutionally scarce resources (core city residency, state enterprise campus hiring, direct doctoral placement).
- The switching cost is structurally prohibitive: the system does not provide re-entry mechanisms, making the cost of exploration catastrophically high — a deliberate disincentive to non-linear trajectories.

## 3. Current Reform Trends and Structural Limitations

### 3.1 Incremental Adjustments
Since 2022, some provincial governments (Guangdong, Zhejiang) have extended fresh-graduate protections to "within 3 years of graduation" and relaxed some technical specialist civil service caps to 40.

### 3.2 Persistent Structural Gaps
- The 35-year core cutoff for standard positions remains unchanged at the national regulatory level.
- No dedicated age discrimination enforcement agency or tribunal exists.
- Private employer ATS-based age filtering algorithms face **zero legal scrutiny, disclosure requirements, or compliance audits** under current PRC law.
