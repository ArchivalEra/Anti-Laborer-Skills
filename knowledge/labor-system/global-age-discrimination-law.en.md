# Global Age Discrimination Law Comparative Analysis

## 1. United States: Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA, 1967)

### 1.1 Legislative Origin and Core Provisions
The ADEA (1967, effective 1968) is among the earliest national statutes providing systematic labor market protection against age discrimination:

**Core Protection Framework**:
- Prohibits employers (20+ employees), labor organizations, and employment agencies from discriminating against workers **aged 40 and above** in hiring, promotion, compensation, training, or termination.
- Prohibits age-preferential language in job postings or specifications.
- Establishes worker rights to file administrative complaints with the EEOC and pursue civil litigation.

### 1.2 Empirical Economic Effects
Neumark & Stock (1999) found:
- States introducing age discrimination protections saw **statistically significant employment rate increases** for 40–65 year-olds (approximately 4–6 percentage points).
- Age discrimination protections reduce **premature forced depreciation of human capital**, with measurable positive macroeconomic contributions.

### 1.3 ADEA Limitations
- Minimum protected age of 40 — leaves the 25–39 cohort (primary victims of the Chinese "35-year barrier") unprotected.
- Burden of proof remains challenging for claimants; litigation success rates are moderate.
- Specific industry exceptions (mandatory pilot retirement ages) permitted.

## 2. European Union: Employment Equality Directive (2000/78/EC)

### 2.1 EU Framework
The EU Employment Equality Framework Directive (2000/78/EC) explicitly enumerates **age** as a protected characteristic, requiring all member states to transpose it into national law by 2003.

**Key Provisions**:
1. **Principled prohibition**: Direct age-based differential treatment in hiring, promotion, and termination is presumptively unlawful.
2. **Proportionality justification**: Member states may allow narrow exceptions where the age distinction is justified by a **legitimate aim** pursued through **proportionate and necessary** means (e.g., physical fitness requirements for emergency services).
3. **Mandatory retirement ages**: ECJ rulings mandate that compulsory retirement ages satisfy genuine employment policy objectives, not mere financial or managerial convenience.

### 2.2 Landmark ECJ Cases
- **Mangold v. Helm (C-144/04, 2005)**: German legislation permitting unlimited fixed-term contracts for workers over 52 ruled incompatible with EU equality principles.
- **Heyday (2009) / UK Default Retirement Age Abolition (2011)**: UK mandatory retirement age at 65 subjected to stricter proportionality scrutiny; ultimately abolished.

## 3. Global Jurisdiction Comparison

| Jurisdiction | Age Discrimination Framework | Protected Age Range | Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Australia** | Age Discrimination Act (2004) | All ages | Full bidirectional age protection; covers hiring, promotion, termination |
| **Canada** | Canadian Human Rights Act / provincial statutes | Generally all ages | Most provinces prohibit mandatory retirement; employer must demonstrate justification |
| **Japan** | Comprehensive Employment Policy Law | Partial (45+) | Policy encouragement (not mandate) to hire age 65+; enforcement flexibility significant |
| **South Korea** | Act Prohibiting Age Discrimination in Employment (2009) | 55+ | Public sector mandatory retirement raised to 60; mid-career (35–55) discrimination remains acute |
| **Mainland China** | No dedicated age discrimination legislation | None | Labor Law excludes age from anti-discrimination provisions; 35-year barrier entrenched in both regulation and market practice |

## 4. Comparative Conclusions

Mainland China stands as one of very few major economies with **no dedicated age discrimination protection statute**. The causal chain connecting this institutional gap to measured economic consequences — premature waste of crystallized intelligence peak-period workers, premature exploitation failure in multi-armed bandit optimization, and macroeconomic deadweight loss — constitutes a central empirical finding of this knowledge domain.
